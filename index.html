Research

MSc thesis

What the project asked, how it was set up, and the methods I carried out.

Cardiovascular ageing phenotype and glyoxalase-1 (GLO1) activity in a C57BL/6J mouse model

CARIM (Cardiovascular Research Institute Maastricht), Department of Internal Medicine, Maastricht University · Sep 2025 to Jun 2026

Glucose metabolism→ Methylglyoxal (MGO)→ AGEs→ Vessel stiffening, inflammation
GLO1detoxifies MGO before it can form AGEs

Methylglyoxal is a reactive by-product of glucose metabolism. When it builds up, it forms advanced glycation end-products (AGEs), which stiffen blood vessels and drive inflammation. The body's main defence against it is the enzyme GLO1.

The project asked whether aged mice show a measurable cardiovascular ageing phenotype, and whether that phenotype goes along with changes in GLO1 activity in specific tissues.

Group 2Aged18-month-old male C57BL/6J mice

  1. In vivo measurements Observed

    Echocardiography, contrast-enhanced ultrasound and hyperinsulinaemic-euglycaemic clamp studies, which produced the imaging data I then analysed.

  2. Image analysis Performed

    Validated 200+ datasets before analysis. Measured cardiac structure and function, pulse propagation velocity and aortic distensibility in VevoLab. Analysed contrast-enhanced ultrasound sequences in ImageJ/Fiji, fitting replenishment curves to estimate microvascular blood volume and flow.

  3. Tissue preparation Performed

    Ground snap-frozen tissue under liquid nitrogen and prepared lysates. Tracked, stored at −80 °C and retrieved 130+ specimens with full traceability.

  4. Protein and enzyme assays Performed

    Quantified protein by BCA assay and measured GLO1 activity spectrophotometrically at 240 nm on a microplate reader, across 150+ tissue samples.

  5. Histology Performed

    Tissue processing, paraffin embedding and sectioning.

  6. Statistics Performed

    All analysis in GraphPad Prism and Excel: Shapiro-Wilk normality testing, ROUT outlier detection, unpaired t-tests or Mann-Whitney tests depending on the distribution, and Pearson correlation with linear regression.

The results of this project are unpublished, so they are not shared here.
